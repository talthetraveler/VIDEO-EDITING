## 0. The one rule: say it, see it

Every line has a picture word: a noun, a place, a number, an action or a person. The clip under that word SHOWS that word, and the cut lands on the word (within ±3 frames), not on the start of the sentence. If no clip shows it, go to Tal on camera or put the word on screen as big text. Never use a "nice" shot that's only loosely related. The references do this on every line (DdO1hvwzXuR: "every claim has a literal picture: flag, soldier, article screenshot"; DXr7nZfkT5z: "B-roll inserts land on the noun being said"; DdB95uZuBL_: "place cards land exactly on the place name").

## 2. VO storytelling rules (each with two real examples)

1. **The picture word gets its literal shot, cut on the word.**
   - De2Gk5gPKNi: "a simple 'hello'" → him saying hi to a stranger; "edit a video… until 2 AM" → him at the desk at night.
   - De6rEXSonQc: "for the first time in my life, put on a dry suit" → him suiting up; "the captain shouted: 'Orcas!'" → selfie with the captain behind.
2. **A list is one shot per item, in the order it's spoken.**
   - DcF1qekqjPy (our build): "free hotels, free food and free tours" → hotel pool → food spread → karst boat tour.
   - DbJk-kXzUJV: six facts, one shot each ("no snakes and no sharks", "no fast food or hotel chains"…). Our DdLg6A0pCvi build does it right on "skateboarded… ate family-style meals… partied… rode above the clouds… stood at the China border": skateboarding → lunch stop → night karaoke → selfie in the clouds → Lung Cu flag tower.
3. **A place name or number gets a card, map, flag or big number that lands on it.**
   - DdLg6A0pCvi: "This is the Ha Giang Loop" → green map card at 9–11 s; "$200" → big yellow "$200" over shot 1.
   - C8mxY7MC7YJ: "no viewpoint on Google Maps" → Google Maps satellite pin at 2.5 s. Dd7b6_-PB4L: "1,200 miles and three islands" → dark satellite map with the route.
4. **Abstract lines get text or Tal's face, never filler.**
   - De2Gk5gPKNi: "this is not about running" → black screen, plain white words, music dips ~10 dB.
   - DcdXddhRRSo: the key line gets one big white word slam ("OF MY"). Tal's rule (Oct 23): no loosely related b-roll under abstract words. Use kinetic text or cut to the talent.
5. **The first sentence is the whole promise, with a number, a danger or a contrast.**
   - DdLg6A0pCvi: "$200. That's what I spent on a four-day motorbike tour…", with a big yellow "$200" over shot 1.
   - DePMdrpI106: "…one of the most dangerous countries in the world, and I did not expect this to happen" over him on the road.
6. **Open on the person, and the first shot is the longest.**
   - Ded4uNDRImA: "This is Alex…", with the one long hold under the hook and a big "ALEX".
   - De2Gk5gPKNi: the twins' selfie holds 2.9 s (the longest shot in the video). DbiiKy4uxEX: a 2.7 s hook to camera, "This is the first time I've been able to come back to Syria in over 15 years."
7. **Short declarative lines, one clause per shot.**
   - Ded4uNDRImA: "Alex is a delusional optimist. I'm a realist…" A small pause between sentences, ~3.5 words/s, a cut on each phrase.
   - DcdXddhRRSo: 102 shots in 171 s, "one shot per clause".
8. **Time connectors drive the story, and the shots follow the clock.**
   - DdLg6A0pCvi: "Getting there… The next morning… Over the next four days…".
   - De6rEXSonQc: "I got up early in the morning… Then, after half an hour…". DcdXddhRRSo: "Three days before… at 7 am… by sunset… at midnight". Morning lines get morning shots, and "after dark" gets a night shot.
9. **Tension comes from the gap between what you expect and what happened, then "But…".**
   - DePMdrpI106: fear in line one, an invite to eat in line three.
   - Dajtw_5gI42: "On paper, [place] is incredible" → "But the more I looked around, the more conflicted I became." De6rEXSonQc: "But then the most extreme thing I could imagine happened."
10. **The payoff is the longest hold, and it's the thing itself.**
   - De6rEXSonQc: "In front of my face was the most dangerous but most beautiful predator" → underwater orca close-up, a 4.3 s hold.
   - DdLg6A0pCvi: "risked my life for this photo" → the drone shot of him on the cliff ledge (the photo itself). Ded4uNDRImA: punchline over a 4.8 s hold.
11. **A quote becomes sync sound or the speaker on screen.**
   - DePMdrpI106: the real reaction, "This is real. This is real.", plays as sync sound.
   - Dcqn_6XokIa (Tal's version): "teach you to wrestle: 'grab the leg'" → aa72e4cd, the man saying it.
12. **Captions are the VO, 1–2 words at a time. Only the key word goes big, and it's on screen with its picture.**
   - Ded4uNDRImA: big RYAN / OPPOSITES / ONCE A YEAR / EGYPT / 15 HOURS, each over that shot.
   - DcF1qekqjPy: "Me." → a handwritten "ME" arrow pointing at Tal grinning. De2Gk5gPKNi: footnote captions as receipts ("*commercial I shot for GMC*").
13. **Examples come in pairs ("Like… / Or…"), each with its own place's footage.**
   - Ded4uNDRImA: "Like last year, when… / Or this year, when…".
   - Tal's Ryan version: "Like in Egypt…" over Egypt road footage, "Or in Pakistan…" over the Pakistan hitchhike.
14. **End on a lesson that calls back to the hook, then one CTA line or a cliffhanger.**
   - DdLg6A0pCvi: "…easily the best $200 I've ever spent. If you get to Hanoi, go north."
   - Dajtw_5gI42: the opening quote comes back ("yeah, for you"). De6rEXSonQc: "You won't believe what you'll see in the next video. I promise you, hit follow."
15. **The music sits steady ~12 dB under the voice.** The only gap is one 0.4–0.9 s silence before the final line or the drop (De2Gk5gPKNi, Tal's −14 LUFS rule).

## 4. Shot-matching checklist (editors run this on every line)

Make a sheet before cutting: | time | line | picture word(s) | shot type | clip id | ✔ |

1. **Underline the picture word(s)** in the line: noun, place, number, action, person.
2. **Find the clip that shows that exact thing**, ideally the clip where Tal says it on camera (search files/transcripts/<Country>.md for the words).
3. **Cut on the word onset (±3 frames)**, not at the start of the line. Two picture words in one line means two shots.
4. **List: one shot per item**, in the spoken order.
5. **Place name or number:** a map card, flag, place card or big number lands on the word.
6. **"I felt / I realised" / abstract words:** Tal's face on camera, or a black card with the key word. Never scenery filler.
7. **Quote:** sync sound, or the person saying it on screen.
8. **Same trip, same day, same time of day** as the line: no Vietnam drone under a Pakistan line, no daylight under "after dark", no group already on the train under "we waited by the tracks".
9. **Payoff word gets the longest hold.** No shot used twice, no identifiable kids, sharp and stable only.
10. **Mute test:** play it with captions only. Every caption has to describe what's on screen. If a shot could be swapped for any other drone shot without anyone noticing, it fails. Re-cut it.

**How the tension works**
- **Three open loops, closed in reverse order.** Question 1 opens at 0 s (how?) and closes at 92 s. The promise opens at 9 s (contacts) and closes at 101 s. Question 2 opens at 33 s (where is the real train?) and closes at 70 s. At any moment at least one loop is open.
- **Obstacles stack, and each is a person or a rule.** The order is: no contacts, then a stop you must get off at, then a man who only talks if paid, then hiding, then a contact waiting at one exact spot. Each one is said in a single line and never explained further.
- **Releases are cuts to daylight.** Beat 5 comes after the first night and beat 8 after the ambush. Both are colour, wide shots and slower words.
- **The slow moment is the gatekeeper, not the view.** The longest hold sits on the man who decides whether the story can go on. The scenery never gets a hold longer than 2.9 s.
- **The picture and the voice move against each other.** In beat 6 the picture is slowest and the voice fastest. In beat 8 the voice slows while the picture opens up. The cut rate stays at about one shot per 1.3 s almost everywhere. The changes in feel come from the speech rate and the light, not from the editing pace.

**Text, only as far as it serves the story**
- Captions show one word at a time: small white lowercase semi-bold sans, centred about 71% down, no box. At 4 words/s each word is on screen for about 0.25 s. They are for sound-off reading only and never compete with the picture.
- The hook alone gets big text: yellow highlight blocks that blur in, phrase by phrase ("and this is part two / of how you can / do this trip / you too"), with the "Part 2" title pinned at the top.
- Place names arrive as phone-map screenshots with a red pin and a white label, and as a pin label over live footage. The map is the establishing shot.
- The end card repeats the call to action in the same yellow highlight style.

**The recipe, reusable for any quest story with footage**
1. **Hook (≤5 s):** the last action of the story, plus a superlative, plus a promise to the viewer. Bring the same shot back in beat 9.
2. **Wall (≈7 s):** "without ___ it's practically impossible." Promise something the last line will deliver.
3. **First leg (≈15–18 s):** a map pin on the place name, 3–5 sensory items with one shot each, and one rule with a consequence.
4. **Fork (≈9 s):** "two options: the easy one, or what we did," and reveal that the real prize is still ahead. This plants the second question.
5. **Side quests (≈15 s):** 4–6 items, one shot each, in daylight. The fun before the dark.
6. **Gatekeeper (≈8 s):** the one person who controls access, the price or condition in one line, and the longest hold of the film on his face or hands.
7. **Ambush (≈7 s):** everything at once in the dark. The voice is at its fastest, the shots near black, and the music drops out under it.
8. **Reveal (≈12 s):** cut to light on "and now…". The widest shot, the voice slowing down, the music at its loudest. This pays the second question.
9. **Arrival (≈14 s):** one last rule, the hook's action again in context, then faces. The emotional last line goes here.
10. **Pay the promise (≈9 s):** the call to action over a recap of the best shots at about 1 s each.

**Where it differs from the rest of this file**
- It is longer (104 s, against the 45/60 s template in section 3).
- It is faster (4.07 words/s, against the 3.4 the template assumes).
- It has no silent gap. Rule 15 asks for one 0.4–0.9 s silence; this reel instead drops the music under the ambush. Both are measurements of other creators, not of Tal's own work, so neither overrides the other. Pick per story.
- It uses no live line at all. Where a story's footage has a strong live line from the gatekeeper or from the dark stretch, beats 6 and 7 are the place for it. That is our choice, not the reference's.
