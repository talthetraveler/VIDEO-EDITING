## What it is

Tal crossing a place with no money, relying on strangers for a ride, a meal, a
bed. It is the same show as the rest of his channel — **a stranger's face
changing when they're treated well** — except here the kindness flows toward
him, and the constraint (no money) is what makes every yes mean something.

This is his own backstory, already in his manifesto film: *"hitchhiked"* ->
back of a desert truck; *"fed me / drove me / let me sleep"* -> food on the
floor / a motorbike / asleep (`voiceover-broll.md` §1). **[S]**

## The journey story — structure

**Stakes -> the ask -> the answer -> the kindness -> the payoff -> where next.**

1. **Stakes in the first 1.5s.** The constraint and the goal in one line or
   one image: *no money, and I need to get to X / eat / sleep tonight.* Open
   inside the situation, never on a walking shot or a skyline. **[S]** (hook
   rule, CLAUDE.md §2.6)
2. **The ask.** Keep Tal's actual question — the viewer has to hear what he's
   asking for, or the yes means nothing. A line and its reply are one unit. **[S]**
3. **Uncertainty is allowed, negativity is not.** A "no" or a hesitation may
   stay briefly as tension — his standard is *uncertainty first, shared
   humanity next, then a warm payoff*. **Never** mock, linger on, or caption
   someone who says no; nobody who turns him down is the villain. **[S]** /
   how many no's to keep: **[?]**
4. **The yes and the kindness.** The ride, the plate, the bed. Let the
   reaction shot breathe. Show the thing given — every promise in the dialogue
   is paid off on screen. **[S]**
5. **Payoff.** The human moment, not the logistics: the conversation in the
   car, the family at the table, what they said about why they helped. Look
   for the second beat — kindness passed on — it is the ending. **[S]**
   (`two-camera-kindness`: the free drink carried out to a street cleaner)
6. **Where next.** A short closing beat that sets up the next episode. **[?]**

## Failure states

- Opening on the road, the backpack, a sunset, or a map instead of a face or the stakes
- A montage of "yes" moments with no ask — the constraint disappears and it's a travel reel
- Dropping the ask to save time, so the kindness has no setup
- Making someone who said no look bad
- Logistics as the ending ("and then I got to the next city") instead of the human moment
- Turning it into a hardship/poverty story — it's about the people, not about being broke
