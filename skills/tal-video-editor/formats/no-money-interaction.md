## The recipe

1. **One real exchange with one person.** Offer, refuse, insist, warmth. The strongest beat is someone refusing his money.
2. **Open already inside it.** First frame is the person, the hand, the thing. His first words are on the first frame.
3. **Long shots.** 2-12 s each. Cuts only remove dead air, and the sound of the moment carries it. Never a montage, no b-roll layered over talk.
4. **Title on top for the whole reel or the first 4 s.** It names the viewer or a tension, with the country and flag:
   - his: `pov: you are a tourist in Jordan 🇯🇴`
   - Arthur's: `Who said Egypt is full of Scammers? 🇪🇬` in black bold on a white pill, held to the end
5. **The caption is the post's second title.** "Don't be scared to travel" is his best one.
6. **Captions:** white, lower-middle, 1-4 words. Translate what locals say. Arthur colours the local's lines yellow so two voices read apart. Keep the local's own broken English word for word: it's the charm.
7. **A small joke caption is allowed:** `kid spawns in*`. An end word can land the idea: `JORDANIAN HOSPITALITY`.
8. **Natural colour, clean speech.** Reduce wind and background noise under the talk (Tal, 2026-11-02), keeping voices natural. Raise the local's voice, because he holds the camera.
9. **End on the warmest second.** A hug, a thank-you, food being shared. No call to action.

## The negotiation reel (Tal, 2026-11-02)

*"Important to highlight is where I negotiate, like getting things for free. Going up to restaurants saying, hey, I'll make you a video. And then they say no, no. The rejection, and then getting one saying yes. That's important."*

- Shape: the ask, a no (or several no's cut tight), then the yes and what he gets. The no's make the yes land.
- Show the real pitch in his words: "I'll make you a video", "in exchange for", "for free".
- Sources: iPhone clips, GoPro vlogs, and his old long-form edits. In a long form, find the best stretch and lift only that.
- Keep each no long enough to sting, a second or two of the face, then cut.

## Framing a 360 clip (Tal, 2026-11-02)

*"You can reframe it to be vertical, reframe it to be horizontal... usually for reels it's vertical, for my long form episodes horizontal. You could choose how zoomed in you want it on my face... if I'm speaking to someone, it could be on me, and then to him."*

- Reels 9:16, long form 16:9, from the same `.insv`.
- The camera is a director: aim at whoever is talking, Tal or the other person, and follow them as they move. Turn to the other person on their first word, back to Tal on his.
- Zoom by the moment: close on a face for a line that matters, wide for a place or an arrival.
- The tool's default aim is a start. Set the aim per beat from the panorama, then check it on frames.
- Always start from the raw `.insv`, never a flattened `temp_video_*.mp4` export, unless no raw exists (LESSONS 178).

**Framing on these reels.** This overrides the "never crop" rule for this format only. Tal: *"the footage maybe needs to be rotated, sometimes they can be cropped, it doesn't need to be vertical but like horizontal like Arthur did, that's ok."*

- Crop when the person stays in frame and it stays sharp.
- If a crop would lose people, keep the clip horizontal in the middle of the 9:16 frame with a blurred copy of itself above and below, as Arthur does, and the white pill title on the top band.

## The Nolan Saumure version

Tal: *"so simple."* Same bones, one change: **a sentence of talk to camera sets up the moment**, in his own words, live or dropped over the footage. "Apparently inside this floating house is a barber." "So our entrance into the temple comes with a stick." Then the moment plays. His dry reactions are the jokes. Nothing is scripted to a template.

It suits places Tal walked into: a strange job, a local trade, a thing he'd never seen. His live line beats a recorded voiceover. Only use a voice-over line if he has no line, and only with his yes.

## Choosing clips

- **Meta glasses first** for POV, with his hand in frame. Then the GoPro vlog folders. Tal: *"especially my vlogs, in Oman, getting in the car."*
- Search the transcripts for refusals and offers: "no money", "for free", "my friend", "welcome", "you are our guest", "habibi", "how much".
- Check the whole clip for the person's face being sharp and warm.
- Same moment, new title, can run again: `DXVM1dZCKcF` and `DX-C4j0gpY9` are the same cut. Ask him before reposting.
- Check `PRIVATE-RULES.md` and his no-go list before using any clip: a clip's talk can rule it out even when the moment is warm. No identifiable kids in close shots without his yes.
